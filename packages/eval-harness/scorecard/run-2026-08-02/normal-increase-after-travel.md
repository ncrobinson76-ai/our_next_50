# normal-increase-after-travel

_Safety check passed; normal synthesis below._

### Recorded Facts

- Weight was logged every day from 2026-07-13 (168 lb) to 2026-07-19 (168.9 lb).
- Weight rose from 168 lb on 7/13 to a peak of 170.1 lb on 7/17, then declined to 168.9 lb by 7/19.
- Sleep hours were 7 (good) on 7/13, 5.5 (poor) on 7/14, 6 (fair) on 7/15, 5 (poor) on 7/16, 6 (fair) on 7/17, 7.5 (good) on 7/18, and 8 (good) on 7/19.
- Average sleep for the week was 6.43 hours; average hunger level was 4.43.
- Runs (35, 30, and 40 minutes, moderate intensity) were logged on 7/13, 7/18, and 7/19; no activity was logged on 7/14–7/17.
- Meals on 7/14–7/16 included airport/airplane food, hotel buffet breakfasts, rehearsal lunch and dinner, and wedding reception/dinner items with wine, generally higher in estimated calories (600–1100) than meals logged on 7/13, 7/18, and 7/19 (320–550).
- Free-text notes describe 7/14 as a travel day with flight delays, 7/15 as featuring 'very salty restaurant food,' and 7/16 as a wedding day with dancing but also more eating and drinking than usual.
- The weekly reflection states the user traveled for a wedding, attributes the scale increase to salty food, less sleep, and reduced running, and reports the weight 'already coming back down' after returning home.
- Self-reported adherence was 'medium,' self-reported mood was 'relaxed about it,' with mood tags 'aware,' 'unbothered,' and 'back on track.'
- No prior experiment or medication context was provided.

### Observations

- Weight increased by 2.1 lb from 7/13 to a peak on 7/17, then decreased by 1.2 lb over the final two days, ending the week 0.9 lb above the starting value.
- Days with the highest estimated calorie meals (7/14–7/16) coincided with no logged runs and with poor or fair sleep quality.
- Days with home-cooked meals and logged runs (7/13, 7/18, 7/19) had the lowest weight readings and the best sleep quality of the week.
- The rise and partial reversal in weight tracks closely with the travel window described in the reflection and free-text notes.

### Tentative Hypotheses

- The mid-week weight increase may be related to higher sodium and calorie intake from restaurant, airport, and wedding meals rather than fat gain.
- Reduced sleep during the travel days (5–6 hours) may have contributed to fluid retention or the perceived weight increase.
- The pause in running during travel may have reduced typical activity-related fluctuations, though this alone likely accounts for only part of the change.
- The downward trend on the final two days may reflect a return to normal hydration, sodium, and eating patterns rather than a change in body composition.

### What's Working

- Home-cooked meals and moderate-intensity runs were resumed immediately upon returning home (7/18–7/19), coinciding with the lowest weights and best sleep of the week.
- The user maintained a calm, non-reactive stance toward the temporary weight increase, correctly identifying travel-related factors rather than treating it as a setback.
- Sleep quality and duration improved quickly after returning home (7.5 and 8 hours, both 'good').

### Friction

- Running was not logged on four consecutive days (7/14–7/17) during travel, breaking the normal 3x/week pattern.
- Sleep was reduced and lower quality on three of the four travel days, which may have compounded other travel-related effects.
- Meal options were more limited and higher in estimated calories during travel (airports, hotel buffets, rehearsal and wedding events), consistent with the user's own account of reduced control over food choices.

### What Should Remain Unchanged

- The user's normal home routine of cooking most meals and running 3x/week, which they report resuming and which coincides with the weight trend starting to reverse.

### Proposed Next Step (no-change)

The data is consistent with a temporary, travel-related weight fluctuation that is already reversing (170.1 lb peak on 7/17 down to 168.9 lb by 7/19) alongside the return of normal sleep, home-cooked meals, and running. No adjustment is indicated; continuing the pre-travel routine and monitoring the next few days of morning weights should clarify whether the value fully returns to the pre-travel baseline (~168 lb).
