# poor-sleep-high-hunger

_Safety check passed; normal synthesis below._

### Recorded Facts

- Morning weight was logged all 7 days, from 172.6 lb (7/13) to 172.7 lb (7/19), a net change of +0.1 lb over the week.
- Sleep was logged as 'poor' quality on 7/13–7/17, with hours of 5.2, 4.8, 5.5, 5, and 4.5 respectively.
- Sleep quality shifted to 'fair' on 7/18 (6.5 hours) and 7/19 (7 hours).
- Average sleep across the week was 5.5 hours; average hunger level was 7.67.
- On 7/13, 7/14, and 7/16, breakfast was coffee only or a very small item (100–350 kcal), followed by afternoon hunger levels of 8–9.
- On 7/18 and 7/19, breakfast included eggs/toast and pancakes (380–420 kcal), with afternoon hunger levels of 6 and 5 respectively.
- Late meals (8:30pm–10:00pm) were logged every day, including 'extra snacking' on 7/14 and a 'late snack, chips and dip' on 7/17.
- No activity was logged on 7/13–7/17; a 20-minute low-intensity walk was logged on 7/18 and a 30-minute low-intensity walk on 7/19.
- The weekly reflection states adherence was self-reported as 'low,' mood as 'exhausted, stressed,' with mood tags including 'exhausted,' 'stressed,' and 'relieved by the weekend.'
- The reflection notes a work deadline was submitted Friday (7/17), after which 'things calmed down a bit.'
- No prior experiment or medication context was provided in this packet.

### Observations

- Poor sleep quality (5.2 hours or less on 4 of 5 nights) coincided with the five days showing the highest logged hunger levels (8–9).
- The two days with fair sleep quality and longer sleep duration (6.5–7 hours) also had the lowest logged hunger levels (5–6) and the only logged physical activity.
- Days with minimal or skipped breakfast (7/13, 7/14, 7/16) were followed by afternoon hunger levels of 8 or 9.
- Days with a more substantial breakfast (7/18, 7/19) were followed by lower afternoon hunger levels (5–6).
- Late evening meals (after 8:30pm) were present on every logged day, with the two highest-calorie late meals (850 kcal, 700 kcal+snack) occurring during the lowest-sleep days.
- Weight fluctuated within a narrow band (172.6–173.2 lb) and ended the week only 0.1 lb higher than it started, despite self-reported low adherence and elevated hunger.

### Tentative Hypotheses

- The pattern suggests hunger levels this week may be related to short sleep duration, though the data cannot confirm sleep as the sole driver given the concurrent work stress.
- Skipping or minimizing breakfast may be tentatively associated with higher afternoon hunger on the days it occurred, but this is based on only a handful of paired observations.
- The work deadline and associated stress may have contributed to both reduced sleep and increased hunger/intake, as suggested by the reflection, though the evidence packet cannot isolate stress from sleep as separate factors.
- The near-flat weight trend despite reported increased intake may reflect measurement noise, short-term fluid shifts, or a lag between intake changes and scale weight — the data doesn't allow a firm conclusion either way.

### What's Working

- On the two days with more sleep (6.5–7 hours), the user ate a more substantial breakfast, reported lower hunger levels, and logged a walk — suggesting this combination is worth watching as a possibly favorable pattern.
- Weight remained relatively stable across the week even during a high-stress, low-adherence period, which may reflect some underlying resilience in overall eating patterns.

### Friction

- Sleep was poor (under 5.5 hours) on 5 of 7 nights, self-described as the 'worst sleep of the week' on 7/17.
- Breakfast was skipped or minimal on 3 of 7 days, each followed by high afternoon hunger (8–9).
- Late-night eating, including extra snacking, occurred on multiple days and was highlighted in the user's own notes.
- The user self-reported low adherence and described feeling 'exhausted, hungry all day, hard to focus' during the deadline period.

### What Should Remain Unchanged

_None._

Over the next 7 days, log a consistent breakfast (similar to the 380–420 kcal breakfasts eaten on 7/18–7/19) along with nightly sleep hours and afternoon hunger level (0–10). This will help clarify whether the lower afternoon hunger seen on the two days with both a fuller breakfast and more sleep this week tends to hold when one or both factors are present, without drawing conclusions from this week's limited pairings alone.
